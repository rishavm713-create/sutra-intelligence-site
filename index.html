01 · Philosophy and vision

An ancient intellectual heritage.
A modern engineering question.

What does it mean to know? How do we distinguish evidence from assumption, intention from action, and a completed task from a claimed success?

India's philosophical traditions have examined knowledge, reasoning, perception, consciousness, interpretation, duty and action through many schools of thought. They do not all agree—and that diversity is part of their intellectual richness.

SŪTRA draws inspiration from this heritage to ask contemporary engineering questions. The connection is philosophical, not a claim that ancient texts describe modern AI or software.

Vedānta

Questions of consciousness, reality and the changing and unchanging. An inspiration for the project's metaphor of a stable objective amid changing activity.

Nyāya

Knowledge, inference, evidence and the examination of claims. An inspiration for provenance and verification.

Yoga

Discipline, attention and purposeful practice. An inspiration for restraint and controlled execution.

Sāṃkhya

A philosophical distinction between Puruṣa and Prakṛti. A conceptual prompt, not a software equivalence.

Mīmāṃsā

Interpretation, context, injunction and action. An inspiration for careful instruction handling.

Buddhist & Jain thought

Analyses of cognition and inference, and many-sided approaches to complex claims. An inspiration for intellectual humility.

“अहं ब्रह्मास्मि” — Ahaṃ brahmāsmi
A Mahāvākya in the Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 1.4.10. Its meaning belongs to its Upaniṣadic and Vedāntic context.

These traditions are sources of intellectual inspiration, not proof of SŪTRA's technical claims. Read the philosophical foundations and references →

02 · Intelligence architecture

A control layer around the model.

SŪTRA is not a foundation model and not a chatbot. It is being developed as a coordinating layer around models, tools and the work they perform.

Instruments
Energy
Witness
Stillness

01 · Stillness — objective

The user's goal, success criteria and constraints: the reference against which work is assessed.

02 · Witness — records

Records of what tools actually reported doing, distinguished from a model's narrative about its actions.

03 · Energy — planning and control

Planning, coordination, bounded retries, adaptation and recovery.

04 · Instruments — models and tools

Replaceable AI models, deterministic software, APIs and other capabilities.

This is a public conceptual model, not a disclosure of private implementation details. The metaphor does not imply that the philosophical concepts are technically identical to software components.

03 · Core capabilities

What the architecture is built to do.

01

Keep the objective fixed

Compare work against the original goal and identify drift. Changes to the goal remain under user authority.

02

Gate consequential actions

Respect action scope, permissions and approval boundaries; unknown actions should fail safely.

03

Ground facts in records

Distinguish tool records from model statements and preserve the origin of information.

04

Separate doing from verifying

Distinguish attempted, completed and verified work. A claim is not proof of completion.

05

Plan with dependencies

Break complex work into steps, track dependencies and identify blockers.

06

Recover within bounds

Classify failures, avoid identical repeated attempts and escalate when human judgement is needed.

04 · Execution lifecycle

One loop. Six stages.

संकल्प · Saṅkalpa

Anchor

Establish the objective, constraints and success criteria.

विवेक · Viveka

Interpret

Understand the task, relevant context and boundaries.

योजना · Yojanā

Plan

Organise steps, dependencies and suitable capabilities.

कर्म · Karma

Execute

Carry out permitted actions and record outcomes.

प्रमाण · Pramāṇa

Verify

Check results against independent evidence and criteria.

प्रतिफल · Pratiphala

Evaluate

Report honestly, classify failures and preserve lessons.

Sanskrit labels are conceptual motifs, not claims that this six-stage software lifecycle is found in a classical text.

07 · The founder
RM

Rishav Mishra

Founder & Architect · SŪTRA INTELLIGENCE

I started building Sūtra because I believe artificial intelligence needs more than intelligence. It needs discipline, accountability and a reliable relationship with truth.

As AI systems become more capable of reasoning, planning and acting through tools, a fundamental engineering question emerges: how do we ensure that what a system intends, what it actually does and what it claims to have achieved remain consistent?

My inspiration also comes from India's intellectual traditions, which have explored knowledge, reasoning, perception, truth, action, consciousness and reality for centuries. Sūtra is my attempt to bring some of those enduring questions into the engineering of modern AI systems.
